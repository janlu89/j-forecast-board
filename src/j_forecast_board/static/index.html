Total. The sum of all series (e.g. the whole chain). Bottom-up = sum of the per-series forecasts; direct = Timestral applied to the aggregated total. Errors partly cancel out when summing, so the total is usually much easier to forecast.

Live forecast. Pick a series, a date to forecast from and a horizon: Timestral predicts on the spot (a few seconds on CPU). If the chosen date is in the past, the real values are shown and scored; at the end of the data it is a true forecast of the future.
Press Forecast.
Split test – give the model only part of the history. Choose where the data starts, up to when Timestral may look, and how far it must forecast. Everything after the split is hidden from the model; then the forecast is compared with what really happened, period by period, against simple rules that see exactly the same data. Example: 5 years of data → give 2 years → forecast the other 3.

What-if scenarios. Covariates known in advance (opening days, promotions, holidays, weather forecast…) can be edited for the future of the selected series. Compare the base forecast with the scenario. Steps are counted from the first forecast point (step 0).

Anomaly detection. Short forecasts are replayed over the recent past of the selected series and every point outside the model’s uncertainty band is flagged. With a 98% band about 2% of points fall outside by chance; many more means unusual events (outages, storms, data errors, special promotions…).
Press Detect anomalies.

Order planner – from forecast to decision. A forecast is only useful if it changes what you order. Timestral gives a whole range (quantiles), so you can order e.g. the P90 instead of “forecast + 20% buffer”.
